Red car = fast car. Yellow dot = child. A “close call” is when a child is on the road with a car within a very short distance. A “hit” is when a car actually touches a child (a hit child is knocked sideways and turns red). The faster the car, the more likely a hit is fatal, and a fatally hit child disappears from the simulation.. When a close call happens, the street flashes red with a “CLOSE CALL!” warning and a ring around the child. Both streets start with the same traffic and the same number of children, but on the right street some cars are sent to a bypass road.

How the simulation works

  • Cars appear at random and drive along the road. The traffic slider changes how often.
  • Children start at the apartments (top) and walk to the play area (bottom).
  • Before: children walk straight across anywhere, and cars never stop.
  • After: children walk to the zebra crossing and press the button (wait). The signal turns red, cars stop at the white stop line, and then the children cross.
  • When the children are across, the signal turns green again and the cars go. It stays green for a minimum time so traffic is not blocked, and the “Cars passed” counter lets you compare the two streets.
  • The program counts every close call so the two streets can be compared fairly.
  • On the right street 40% of cars are sent to a bypass road at the top of the picture. The signal also stays red for a short time only, so queues stay short. The “Longest queue” number shows how many cars were stopped at once.
  • When a car hits a child, the chance that it is fatal rises quickly with speed. That is why the 20 km/h zone matters.
