Every car has its own colour. A car with a red outline is moving fast. Yellow dot = child. A “close call” is when a child is on the road with a car within a very short distance. A “hit” is when a car actually touches a child (a hit child is knocked sideways and turns red). The faster the car, the more likely a hit is fatal, and a fatally hit child disappears from the simulation. A “car crash” is when a car runs into the back of the car in front. Crashed cars turn dark red, flash their hazard lights and block the lane for a while until they are cleared. Cars with red rear lights are braking. When a close call or crash happens, the street flashes red with a warning. Both streets start with the same traffic and the same number of children, but on the right street some cars are sent to a bypass road.

How the simulation works

  • Cars appear at random and drive along the road. Each car gets its own random colour. The traffic slider changes how often cars appear.
  • Children start at the apartments (top) and walk to the play area (bottom).
  • Before: children walk straight across anywhere. Most drivers notice and slow down or stop after a short reaction time, but some are distracted and don't react in time.
  • Before: like in real life, drivers sometimes slow down for no clear reason (checking a phone, looking at a shop, a pothole). Careful drivers leave a big gap. Distracted drivers follow close and brake late, so a sudden slowdown or stop can cause a rear-end crash.
  • When two cars crash, both stop, hazard lights flash and the lane is blocked for a while. Cars behind them queue up until the crashed cars are cleared.
  • After: children walk to the zebra crossing and press the button (wait). The signal turns red, cars slow down smoothly and stop behind the red stop line, and then the children cross.
  • After: every car keeps a clear gap to the car in front. When the front car stops at the signal, the cars behind stop with a gap between them instead of bunching up, so there are no rear-end crashes.
  • When the children are across, the signal turns green again and the cars go. It stays green for a minimum time so traffic is not blocked, and the “Cars passed” counter lets you compare the two streets.
  • The program counts every close call and every car crash so the two streets can be compared fairly.
  • On the right street 40% of cars are sent to a bypass road at the top of the picture. The signal also stays red for a short time only, so queues stay short. The “Longest queue” number shows how many cars were stopped at once.
  • When a car hits a child, the chance that it is fatal rises quickly with speed. That is why the 20 km/h zone matters.
